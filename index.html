Rules of Biology

Why Understanding Life Requires a Different Kind of Science

Biology today stands at a paradoxical peak.

Never before have we had such vast experimental data, molecular measurements, and technological sophistication. Yet biology remains weakly predictive, internally contradictory, and fragmented across diseases, pathways, and models.

This is not a failure of effort, intelligence, or tools. It is a failure of foundations.

If life operates in nature, it must operate by rules.

This work presents a reconstruction of biology built on that premise.

The Problem: Biology Without Coherence

Modern biology has accumulated unprecedented data, tools, and technologies. Yet it has not converged toward a compact, predictive, and logically complete framework.

Contradictions are absorbed rather than resolved. Exceptions are cataloged rather than explained. Complexity is accepted as intrinsic rather than interrogated.

As a result, biology progresses without unifying. More data produces more descriptions, not deeper necessity.

Why Rules Are Inevitable

If life operates within nature, it cannot be exempt from logic. Living systems must obey governing constraints, just as physical systems do.

Rules are not pathways or correlations. They are invariant constraints that define what must occur across contexts.

Without explicit rules, biology cannot become predictive, compressible, or internally consistent.

Why This Changes Artificial Intelligence

Artificial intelligence trained on rule-less biology learns correlation, not understanding. Rule-based biology provides natural constraints for interpretable and safe intelligence.
